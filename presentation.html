4. Semantic Similarity (ML)

Approach: Machine learning to detect rephrased/republished content

Key Technique: Cosine Similarity

similarity = (A · B) / (||A|| × ||B||)
Where A and B are sentence embeddings | Score: 0 (different) to 1 (identical)

Implementation Details

  • Model: paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2
  • Threshold: 0.75 similarity score
  • Time Window: 48 hours after article publication
  • Min Text Length: 50 characters

How Cosine Similarity Works

Step 1: Encoding

Text is converted to numerical vectors (embeddings) using a neural network trained on multilingual data

"Війна в Україні" → [0.23, 0.45, -0.12, ...]

Step 2: Comparison

Cosine of the angle between vectors measures semantic similarity

Similar meaning = small angle
Different meaning = large angle

Advantage: Detects paraphrased content that doesn't share exact words

Why Cosine over Euclidean/Manhattan?
Distance metrics (Euclidean, Manhattan) are sensitive to text length — longer messages get larger distances even with same meaning. Cosine measures direction (meaning), not magnitude (length), making it ideal for comparing texts of different sizes.

Example: "Війна" vs "Війна в Україні продовжується" have same topic but Euclidean says "different" (large distance). Cosine correctly says "similar" (same direction).

How the Model Works (Under the Hood)

1
Tokenization: "Росія атакувала Київ" → ["Рос", "##ія", "атаку", "##вала", "Ки", "##їв"]
2
Convert to Numbers: Each token gets a unique ID → [5432, 8721, 3456, ...]
3
12 Neural Network Layers: Each layer understands text deeper (patterns → grammar → meaning)
4
Create Embedding: Combine all word meanings → single vector of 384 numbers
5
Normalize: Scale the vector for fair comparison (like adjusting volume)
6
Compare with Cosine Similarity: Measure angle between two embeddings → Score 0.75+ = similar
Key Insight: The model converts sentences to "meaning fingerprints" (384 numbers). Paraphrases get similar fingerprints even in different languages or with different words.

Model Architecture & Training

Architecture

Base: Transformer (BERT-like)
Layers: 12 encoder layers
Hidden size: 384 dimensions
Parameters: ~33 million
Attention heads: 12
Pooling: Mean pooling

Why It's Fast

Knowledge Distillation:
Teacher model (768d) trains student (384d)
Student learns to mimic teacher's outputs
→ 50% size, 95% accuracy, 2× speed

Training Process (3 Stages)

1. Pre-training
Multilingual corpus (Wikipedia, etc.)
Task: Predict masked words
Learns: Grammar, context, word meanings
2. Fine-tuning for Paraphrases
1M+ paraphrase pairs (AllNLI, STS)
Task: Contrastive learning
Learns: Similar meanings → close embeddings
3. Distillation
Compress from teacher (768d) to student (384d)
Maintains performance, doubles speed

Self-Attention: The Core Mechanism

Goal: Each word understands its context by "looking at" all other words

Example: "Russia attacked Kyiv at night"

For word "attacked":

Query (Q): What information do I need? (attack context)
Key (K): What can other words offer?
Value (V): Actual information from other words

Attention Score = softmax(Q · K / √dimension)

Attention Weights for "attacked"

  • Russia: 0.45 (high - who did it?)
  • attacked: 0.10
  • Kyiv: 0.35 (high - what was attacked?)
  • at: 0.02
  • night: 0.08

Result

Embedding of "attacked" becomes:

0.45 × V(Russia)
+ 0.35 × V(Kyiv)
+ 0.10 × V(attacked)
+ 0.08 × V(night)
+ 0.02 × V(at)

Now "attacked" knows WHO attacked WHAT!

Why It Detects Paraphrases

Text A: "Росія атакувала Київ" | Text B: "Кремль напав на столицю"

How the Model Processes This

Layers 1-3 (Surface): "Росія"/"Кремль" are entities • "атакувала"/"напав" are verbs
Layers 4-8 (Syntax): Subject-Verb-Object structure • [AGENT] [ACTION] [LOCATION]
Layers 9-12 (Semantics): [RUSSIA attacks UKRAINE-CAPITAL] • Similar semantic space → Cosine similarity ~0.85+
Final Embeddings:
A: [0.23, -0.45, 0.67, 0.12, -0.89, ...] • B: [0.25, -0.43, 0.65, 0.15, -0.87, ...] (384 numbers each)
→ Cosine Similarity = 0.87 = Same meaning detected!
